The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
⚔️

As the Cyclops lay sprawled on the ground in a deep, wine-soaked sleep, Odysseus knew the moment had finally come. Quietly, he and four of his bravest men dragged the sharpened, fire-hardened stake out from under the straw where it had been hidden. Together, they drove it straight into the Cyclops's single enormous eye.

The monster leaped up, roaring with unbearable pain. He stumbled blindly around the cave, arms outstretched, grabbing wildly for Odysseus and his men — but they slipped easily out of his reach, since he could no longer see where they were hiding. Outside the cave, the Cyclops's terrible screams echoed through the night, and soon other giants who lived nearby came running to see what had happened.

"Why are you making so much noise?" they shouted to him from outside the sealed entrance. "You're keeping us from sleeping! Is someone attacking you?"

"Noman!" the Cyclops bellowed back in agony. "Noman is trying to kill me!"

The other giants paused, confused. "No man is attacking you?" they called back. "Then go back to sleep!" And with that, they wandered off, leaving the Cyclops groaning alone until morning.

At dawn, the injured Cyclops finally rolled the great boulder away from the cave entrance, feeling his way carefully with his hands. As he herded his flock of sheep and goats outside, he ran his fingers along the back of every single animal that passed him, hoping to catch Odysseus or his men trying to sneak out among the herd.

But Odysseus had already thought of that. He tied his men underneath groups of three sheep each, so that each man clung tightly to the woolly fleece on the animal's belly, hidden completely from view. When the Cyclops felt only the backs of the sheep passing by, he suspected nothing.

Once outside and safely past the blind giant, Odysseus and his men released the sheep, sprinted for their ships, and scrambled aboard. As soon as everyone was safe, the sailors rowed frantically out into open water, putting as much distance as possible between themselves and the furious, wounded monster left behind on the island.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past simple + adverbs of manner
Adverbs of manner (often ending in -ly) describe HOW an action happened.
The Cyclops stumbled blindly around the cave.
Odysseus and his men slipped easily out of his reach.
They rowed frantically out into open water.
💡 Adverbs of manner usually come after the verb they describe.
📌 'So that' — purpose clauses
'So that' explains the PURPOSE or GOAL behind an action.
He tied his men underneath the sheep so that they would be hidden from view.
The Cyclops felt each animal's back so that he could catch any escaping men.
They rowed hard so that they could escape the island quickly.
💡 'So that' is usually followed by a subject and a modal verb (would, could, might).
📌 Reported/embedded questions
Embedded questions use normal word order (subject + verb), not question word order.
The giants asked why he was making so much noise.
They wondered whether someone was attacking him.
The Cyclops didn't realize what 'Noman' truly meant.
💡 Notice there is no question mark and no inverted word order inside an embedded question.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
⚔️
Odysseus used violence — blinding the Cyclops — as a form of justice for his men who were eaten. Do you think revenge is ever truly justified, even against a monster?
🗣️
The trick with the name 'Noman' only worked because of a clever play on words. What does this scene teach us about the power of language and wordplay?
🐑
Odysseus's escape plan depended on understanding exactly how the Cyclops would check the animals. How important is it to study your enemy or problem carefully before acting?
🏝️
Even after escaping the cave, Odysseus and his men were still trapped on a dangerous island with an injured, furious monster. Do you think this escape was truly a 'victory,' or just the next step in a bigger struggle?
