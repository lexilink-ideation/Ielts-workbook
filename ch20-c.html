The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
👁️

Odysseus and his twelve companions crept carefully into the enormous cave — but at first, no one seemed to be home. Inside, they found pens crowded with lambs and baby goats. Along the walls stood shelves lined with enormous cheeses, each one bigger than a man's head, and beneath them sat huge bowls used for milking the animals, each large enough for a grown man to lie down inside.

Seeing all this, Odysseus's men grew terrified. "A giant must live here!" they whispered. "Let's grab some cheese and lambs and hurry back to the ship before he returns!" But Odysseus refused to run away. Curious and stubborn, he insisted, "We'll wait here and greet him properly when he comes home." So the men cut some cheese for their dinner, offered a small piece to the gods, and settled in to wait.

As darkness fell over the island, the ground itself began to shake with heavy footsteps. Through the cave entrance walked its owner — a giant as tall as three men standing on each other's shoulders, with a single enormous eye in the middle of his forehead. He was a Cyclops!

The Cyclops herded his flock of sheep in ahead of him and dropped an armful of trees onto the cave floor for firewood, making a noise so loud that Odysseus and his men hid in terror at the back of the cave. Once every animal was safely inside, the Cyclops rolled a massive boulder across the cave entrance — a stone so heavy that twenty grown men together couldn't have moved it. Only then did he light his fire.

When the flames roared up, the Cyclops spotted the strangers hiding in the shadows. "Well, well," he growled, "what do we have here? Thieves, come to steal my sheep and cheese?" Trembling, Odysseus explained that they were simply travelers hoping for hospitality, and — afraid the monster might destroy their ships — he lied, claiming their vessel had already been wrecked.

The Cyclops said nothing in reply. Instead, he snatched up two of Odysseus's men and devoured them on the spot, washing the horrible meal down with goat's milk before falling asleep on the cave floor. "Let's kill him now, while he sleeps!" the terrified men begged. But Odysseus refused. "If we kill him," he reasoned, "who will move that boulder? We would be trapped in here forever." So the men huddled together at the back of the cave, listening all night to the monster's thunderous snoring, waiting for a plan to save them.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Past continuous for background action
Past continuous describes an action in progress at a certain moment in the past.
Odysseus's men were hiding in terror at the back of the cave.
While the Cyclops was sleeping, the men were planning their escape.
The fire was roaring as the Cyclops discovered his guests.
💡 Use past continuous alongside past simple to show what was happening 'in the background.'
📌 First conditional — real future possibility
First conditional = If + present simple, ... will/might + base verb.
If we kill him, who will move the boulder?
If we run, the Cyclops will catch us.
If we wait quietly, we might find a way to escape.
💡 First conditional describes a realistic future result, not an imaginary one.
📌 Reported speech with 'insisted / begged / explained'
Verbs like insist, beg, and explain introduce someone's meaning without using their exact words.
Odysseus insisted that they should wait and greet the owner.
His men begged him to kill the Cyclops while he slept.
Odysseus explained that they were simply travelers.
💡 These reporting verbs each carry a different feeling — insisting is firm, begging is desperate.

Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
👁️
Curiosity led Odysseus into great danger. Do you think the benefits of exploring the unknown are usually worth the risks?
🪨
Odysseus made a difficult, logical decision — not killing the Cyclops immediately — because of a bigger problem, the boulder. Do you think it's important to think about long-term consequences even in a crisis?
🏠
The Cyclops seemed to live entirely alone, with no city or laws, unlike the 'civilized' Greeks in Lesson A. What do you think this contrast tells us about the ancient Greek idea of civilization?
🍽️
Guests and hosts had special rules of hospitality in ancient Greece, yet the Cyclops broke every one of them. Why do you think hospitality was considered so important in the ancient world?
