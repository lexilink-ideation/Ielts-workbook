The Story
Read carefully. Notice the bold words.
🍷

When morning came, the Cyclops woke, lit his fire, milked his goats, and — to the sailors' horror — grabbed two more men for his breakfast. After finishing his terrible meal, he rolled away the massive boulder, drove his flock outside, and then carefully rolled the stone back into place behind him, sealing Odysseus and the remaining men inside once again.

All day long, Odysseus's men moaned and wept, certain they would never escape. But Odysseus paced back and forth across the cave floor, thinking hard. Finally, his eyes fell on the pile of trees the Cyclops had gathered for firewood. One long branch, still green, stood out among the rest.

"Come on," Odysseus told his men. "Be brave. Do exactly as I say, and we will escape this place." Together they cut a long piece from the branch — about as tall as a man — and sharpened one end into a fine point. Odysseus then held the sharpened tip in the fire's coals until it hardened and turned black, before hiding the weapon under a pile of straw. All day, the men waited nervously for the Cyclops to return.

That evening, the monster came back exactly as before, driving his sheep inside and sealing the boulder shut. Once again, he grabbed two more men and devoured them, washing the meal down with goat's milk. This time, however, Odysseus stepped forward, carrying the jug of sweet wine he had brought from the ship.

"Cyclops!" he called out. "You've eaten so much meat that you must be thirsty. Milk alone won't satisfy that thirst — but I have something better. Try this wine; it's the finest you've ever tasted." The Cyclops sniffed at the jug suspiciously, then took a mouthful, then another, and another, until the entire jug was empty. Delighted and drowsy, the giant asked, "What's your name, stranger? Who is giving me such wonderful wine?"

"My name is Noman," Odysseus answered calmly.

"Well, Noman," the Cyclops chuckled, his eyes growing heavy, "since I like your wine so much, I'll do you a favor — I'll eat you last of all!" And with that promise, the enormous monster sprawled across the cave floor and fell into a deep, wine-soaked sleep, giving Odysseus and his remaining men exactly the chance they had been waiting for.

Language Focus
Grammar from the story — learn through meaning
📌 Sequencing a plan: 'First... then... finally...'
Sequencing words help listeners follow the exact order of a plan.
First, they cut a branch; then, they sharpened it; finally, they hid it under straw.
First he sniffed the wine, then he drank a mouthful, then another.
First the Cyclops relaxed, then he grew drowsy, and finally he fell asleep.
💡 Sequencing words are essential for explaining any step-by-step plan clearly.
📌 'So + adjective + that' — result clauses
'So + adjective + that' links a quality to its result.
The Cyclops drank so much wine that the whole jug was empty.
He felt so satisfied that he offered Odysseus a strange 'favor.'
The wine was so delicious that he asked for more.
💡 This structure shows a cause strong enough to create a clear effect.
📌 Using a false name for protection
A character can hide their true identity behind a clever, misleading name.
"My name is Noman," Odysseus said — a name with a hidden trick inside it.
The Cyclops promised to eat 'Noman' last, not knowing what the name truly meant.
Sometimes what a character says means something different to the reader than to the other characters.
💡 This kind of wordplay, where the audience understands more than a character does, is called dramatic irony.

8
Ideas & Society
Think about big ideas. There is no wrong answer.
🍷
Odysseus used cleverness, not brute strength, to gain an advantage over a much bigger, stronger enemy. Can you think of other stories, or real-life examples, where cleverness defeats strength?
🎭
The name 'Noman' is a clever trick with a hidden meaning that the Cyclops doesn't understand yet. Why do you think wordplay and hidden meanings are so powerful in storytelling?
🤔
Odysseus stayed patient for an entire day, planning carefully instead of panicking. How do you usually behave when you are in a stressful or frightening situation — do you plan, or react quickly?
⚖️
Is it ever morally acceptable to lie? Odysseus lied about his name and his ship to protect himself and his men. Discuss when, if ever, lying might be justified.
